# Social Virality (v2)

Virality is probabilistic. Never promise it. Optimize the content-audience match and the sequence of attention, payoff, identity, emotion, and social action.

Built from two sources Javi provided: the `100 Viral Formats (Format Book)` Drive folder (100 format specs with example videos, in five families) and the `1000 HOOKS VIRALES Y SUS EJEMPLOS` doc. Both are fully indexed in the references below.

## Operating sequence

1. Read `references/decision-system.md` and choose a job: reach, authority, saves, shares, comments, conversion, or community bonding.
2. Read `references/taxonomy.md` and select one primary format family. Add at most one secondary mechanism.
3. Pick the concrete format: browse `references/format-catalog.md` for the family's numbered formats. Each entry carries the source example, hook pattern, mechanisms, reuse rules, risks, and the format book's full execution notes (text hook, caption, B-roll, music, structure). Watch the example video in the source Drive folder before producing a format for the first time.
4. Read `references/hooks.md`, then draft hooks. For Spanish content, mine `references/hook-library.md`: 1000 categorized hooks with example links. Write 5 materially different hooks and reject any whose promise the body cannot pay off.
5. Read `references/narrative-and-retention.md` and map beats before writing full copy.
6. Read `references/visual-audio-editing.md` and specify what is seen and heard at each beat. Visuals must carry information, tension, proof, or a payoff rather than decorate.
7. Read `references/platform-fit.md` and adapt packaging to the actual platform and creator assets.
8. Score the draft with `references/evaluation-rubric.md`. Revise every red-flag dimension.
9. For medical, financial, legal, safety, or factual claims, verify them independently. Viral source material is not evidence that a claim is true.

## Fit with Javi's video pipeline

This skill covers ideation, format, hook, script structure, and packaging. It complements the video-edit and video-script skills; it does not replace them or change their defaults. In particular:

- TV-1's natural editing stays the default: no forced micro-cuts, expressive pauses kept, minimalism preserved. Catalog formats that demand fast cut-to-the-beat editing (some Easy-to-use entries) are opt-in per video, never the default.
- Use this skill to choose what to make and how to open it; use the editing skills for how the edit feels.
- Music named in the catalog is the source creator's reference. Add known music at upload time in the platform app rather than baking it in, and check availability for the account and region.

## Required output for a new short-form concept

- Objective and audience state.
- Chosen format family and catalog format number, with why it fits.
- Five hooks, ranked with one-line tradeoffs; note the library number for any hook adapted from the 1000-hook doc.
- Recommended hook.
- Beat sheet with estimated timing.
- Script or on-screen text.
- Shot list/B-roll plan.
- Audio and editing plan.
- Caption and CTA, only if the CTA serves the objective.
- Platform adaptation notes.
- Risks, claim checks, and what would make it fail.
- Rubric score with the weakest dimension named.
- Two controlled variants for testing one variable at a time.

## Non-negotiables

- Match specificity to a real audience tension. Broad relevance is not the same as vagueness.
- Put the clearest source of tension, novelty, identity, utility, or unfinished action in the first beat.
- Preserve authenticity. Do not invent credentials, results, personal stories, or proof.
- Do not copy a source creator's script, footage, or distinctive expression. Abstract the mechanism and rebuild it from the user's own experience, evidence, and assets. The format book's execution notes are instructions to follow; the example captions and scripts are not text to reuse.
- Do not force fast cuts. Pace follows information density and emotional intent.
- Do not use comment bait, fake controversy, false scarcity, unsupported absolutes, or misleading before/after claims.
- Do not confuse retention with satisfaction. A delayed payoff that disappoints damages trust.
- If the user's goal, audience, platform, or available assets are unknown and materially change the format, ask. Otherwise state a reasonable assumption.
