# Visual, audio, and editing system

## Give every visual a job

A shot should do at least one:
- Show proof.
- Clarify a mechanism.
- Progress an action.
- Establish place, person, or stakes.
- Create contrast.
- Carry emotion.
- Reset attention after a dense verbal beat.

Decorative B-roll that competes with the message is noise.

## Visual patterns

- Direct camera: trust, opinion, validation. Prioritize eye line, clean audio, expressive delivery.
- Demonstration: utility and proof. Frame hands/object/result clearly.
- Text over B-roll: simple testing. Match action to the line whenever possible.
- Side-by-side: comparison, misconception, check/cross. Make labels unambiguous.
- Screenshot/artifact: authority and evidence. Zoom to the exact evidence; protect private data.
- Unfinished action: fruit cutting, drawing, building, cooking, cleaning, arranging. It must resolve.
- Progression/countdown: one visual state per step; ensure enough reading time.
- Talking head plus inserts: use inserts only where they clarify or prove.

## Captions and typography

- Burned-in captions support silent viewing; they do not replace visual design.
- Highlight meaning, not every word.
- Keep line breaks semantic.
- Maintain strong contrast and safe margins.
- Avoid tiny blocks of paragraph text in the video. Move depth to the caption only when the video gives standalone value.

## Cuts and transitions

Cut on:
- New information.
- A verb/action change.
- A reaction.
- A proof insert.
- A beat in music when it supports the idea.

Do not cut solely because a timer elapsed. Transitions should clarify time, place, comparison, or causality. Flashy transitions that convey none of these are usually a tax.

## Audio

- Voice intelligibility wins. Music sits under speech.
- Original audio can create intimacy and distinctiveness.
- Trend audio is useful only when its emotional meaning and audience association fit.
- Use beat drops for a real reveal, list start, transformation, or energy change.
- Silence or room tone can create weight before an important line.
- Sound effects should confirm action or punctuate structure, not fill every gap.
- Check music rights and platform availability; availability differs by account and region.

## Production levels

Low: one shot, text, clean sound, one idea.
Medium: talking head plus 3-6 proof/B-roll inserts.
High: planned scenes, multiple locations, sound design, motion graphics.

Choose the lowest production level that expresses the mechanism. Polish is not a substitute for clarity.
