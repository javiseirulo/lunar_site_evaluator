# Format catalog (v2, complete)

One entry per format in the 100 Viral Formats format book (Drive folder `100 Viral Formats (Format Book)`), grouped by family: 25 Advice, 25 Opinion, 25 Relatable, 15 Easy to use format, 10 Storytelling. Each entry combines:

- **Source**: the example post the format was distilled from (reference only; never copy its creative expression).
- **Hook pattern**: the on-screen text hook of the source, as a pattern to abstract.
- **Mechanisms / New-use rule / Risk**: cross-format analysis (what makes it work, how to reuse it honestly, where it fails).
- **Execution notes**: the format book's own production instructions - text hook, caption, B-roll, music and structure rules. These are the operational core: follow them when producing the format.
- **Reference music**: track named by the format book when given. Always check rights and availability in the target account and region before use; add known music at upload time rather than baking it in.

The example videos live beside each document in the numbered subfolder of the source Drive folder. Watch the example before producing a format for the first time.

## Example media review (done 2026-11-11)

All 100 example videos and both audio files were pulled from the shared Drive folder and checked. 99 of 100 videos were probed directly (duration, resolution, orientation) and a labeled frame grid per family was visually inspected; the 100th, Storytelling #01's 149 MB video, exceeded the review tooling's 25 MB download cap and was inspected on 2026-11-12 through the Drive web player, with frames captured across its full 68.2 s (findings in its entry below). Every audio track was extracted and checked for speech with automated transcription: 73 are music-only (methodology is entirely on-screen text), 5 use trend/meme vocals, and 21 carry spoken narration - each of those 21 has a one-line gist in its entry (full transcripts reviewed, not reproduced, per the no-copying rule). The two audios are music beds for Storytelling #01 (1:08, one spoken line: "Okay, I love it") and Storytelling #09 (0:44 instrumental); they carry no spoken methodology, they are the reference tracks named in those formats' notes.

Observed visual patterns per family (consistent with each format's documented execution notes):

- **Advice** (avg ~26s, 5-96s): selfie talking-head with a large on-screen hook, demonstrations and screen recordings, list overlays, several "read caption" bridges where the video teases and the caption delivers.
- **Opinion** (avg ~14s, 6-45s): aesthetic or moody b-roll under contrarian/motivational text lines, black screens with serif text hooks, direct-to-camera rants; the opinion is carried by the text, the footage sets the emotional tone.
- **Relatable** (avg ~14s, 4-51s): candid everyday POV scenes (cooking, commuting, studying, gym), timestamps, identity listicles ("7 signs you've..."), deliberately unstaged feel.
- **Easy to use format** (avg ~13s, 5-22s): one static shot or simple loop with a bold text overlay, labeled objects, minimal production; the text does all the work.
- **Storytelling** (avg ~62s, 42-83s): the only long formats - multi-scene narratives with cinematic b-roll, screenshots (notes app, photos), handwriting, timestamped scenes and voice-over or third-person caption narration.

Practical read: 99 of 100 examples are vertical (720x1280 class; Storytelling #01, reviewed 2026-11-12, is an iPhone screen recording of its Instagram post - vertical but taller than 9:16) and the single landscape exception is Opinion #15 (960x720); all rely on on-screen text plus pacing of information rather than editing effects - which keeps this corpus fully compatible with TV-1's natural editing default.

### Advice #01
- Example video (inspected 2026-11-11): 6.2s, 720x1280 vertical.
- Audio track (checked 2026-11-11): no speech - music track only; the methodology lives in the on-screen text and is captured in the execution notes above.
- Source: https://www.instagram.com/p/DUSKhE3E5FL/
- Source hook pattern: Some strange advice from the internet i laughed at until it worked
- Mechanisms: curiosity/contradiction, identity/relatability, authority/proof, visual progression, caption depth, participation CTA.
- New-use rule: Abstract the structure, replace the claim, examples, footage, language, and proof with the creator's own material.
- Risk/counterexample: Verify health/financial/scientific claims and add limits. Rebuild from original assets; do not copy distinctive expression. Compress for on-screen use; keep deep detail in caption or a series.
- Reference music: Papaoutai (Afro Soul)
- Execution notes:
  Execution
  Text Hook
  Keep the text on screen exactly the same as the original video
  Use pieces of advice that are not popular but have genuinely worked for you, the more unusual or niche the advice sounds the more curiosity it creates
  Give a short personal experience for each piece of advice showing that it actually worked for you, this builds trust quickly
  Caption
  Avoid general advice such as sleeping 8 hours or eating whole foods, the advice needs to feel surprising or unfamiliar rather than obvious
  Keep each point to one or two short sentences describing your personal experience and what the advice helps with, avoid overexplaining
  End the caption with a simple call to action inviting viewers to share their thoughts, such as "what do you think?"
  B-roll
  Follow the b-roll from the original video as closely as possible
